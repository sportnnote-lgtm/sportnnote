The scorecard shows when each wicket fell, which pair added the most runs, how the extras were given away, and which over turned the match. After the match, each player's figures go to their cricket profile and the tournament's leaders. This guide explains each part.

## Before you start
- Anyone who can see the match can read its scorecard. You don't need to be signed in as a scorer.
- Open the match and tap the **Scorecard** tab. Tap a team's name to open or close their innings.

## The batting table
- **R** runs, **B** balls faced, **4s** and **6s** boundaries, and **SR** strike rate.
- Under each name is how they got out, for example "c Arjun b Kabir". A * means not out; the bat symbol marks the striker in a live match.
- "Yet to bat" lists the rest of the side. Tap any name to open that player's profile.

## Extras, total and fall of wickets
- **Extras** shows the total and its make-up, for example "12 (b 2, lb 1, wd 6, nb 2, pen 5)".
- **Total** shows runs/wickets, overs and the run rate (CRR).
- **Fall of wickets** lists each wicket in order. "3-45 (Asha, 7.2 ov)" means the 3rd wicket fell at 45 runs, Asha was out, and it was the 2nd ball of the 8th over. Retired hurt isn't a wicket, so it isn't listed.

## The bowling table
- **O** overs, **M** maidens, **R** runs, **W** wickets, **0s** dot balls, and **Eco** economy (runs per over).
- Wides and no-balls bowled are shown under the bowler's name, for example "2wd 1nb".
- A maiden is a complete over by one bowler with no runs charged to them. Wides and no-balls spoil a maiden; byes and leg byes don't.
- If fielding notes were added, a line such as "Fielding: Ravi 1 drop · Asha 6 saved" follows. See [Add penalty, bonus and minus runs, and fielding notes](/guides/cricket-penalty-and-bonus-runs/).

## Partnerships and the overs chart
- Tap **Partnerships** to see each batting pair's runs and balls, with what each batter scored in it. A * means the pair is still batting.
- Tap **Overs** to see a bar for each over's runs. A mark on top of a bar means a wicket fell in that over. Tap a bar to see that over ball by ball, with the bowler and the score at the end of it, for example "Over 7 · Kabir · 9 runs · 54/2".

> **Note:** Very old matches may not show these parts, which come from the ball-by-ball record.

## Career stats on a profile
1. Open a player's profile.
2. Under **By sport**, tap **Cricket**.
3. You'll see **Apps**, the **W-D-L** record and **Win %**, then three sections: **Batting**, **Bowling** and **Fielding**.

**Apps** counts every match the player was in the playing XI for, even without batting or bowling. A tie shows **TIE** and counts under **Ties**. A no result shows **NO RESULT** and isn't part of Win %, which is wins out of the matches that had a result.

What the short names mean:
- **Avg** under Batting: runs divided by the number of times out. Not-outs don't count as an out, so a batter with 120 runs and 3 dismissals averages 40.00.
- **SR** under Batting: runs per 100 balls faced. 30 runs off 20 balls is a strike rate of 150.00.
- **Highest**: the best score. A * means not out.
- **Econ**: runs given away per over, counted in 6-ball overs.
- **Avg** under Bowling: runs given away per wicket. Lower is better.
- **SR** under Bowling: balls bowled per wicket. Lower is better.
- **Best**: the best bowling in one match, for example "3/12": most wickets, then fewest runs.
- Under **Fielding**: Catches, Stumpings, Run outs, and the fielding notes (Drops, Runs saved, Runs missed).

A "–" means there's nothing to work it out from yet, for example a bowling average with no wickets.

> **Tip:** Super Over runs and wickets aren't counted in careers. They're a tie-breaker, not part of the match.

## Tournament records leaders
1. Open the tournament and tap the **Stats** tab.
2. Tap **Cricket**.
3. Swipe the leader cards sideways.

After **Runs**, **Wickets** and **Catches** come the records: **Highest score** (a * means not out), **Best bowling** (most wickets, then fewest runs), **Best batting average**, **Best strike rate**, **Best economy**, **Most 50s** and **Most 100s**.

A rate means little after a few balls, so these cards need a minimum, shown under the card's title:
- **Best batting average**: at least 3 innings, and out at least once.
- **Best strike rate**: at least 30 balls faced.
- **Best economy**: at least 10 overs bowled.

These suit school tournaments of 3 to 5 matches. A card appears once somebody qualifies. Players level on a figure are ordered by more runs (or, for economy, more balls bowled). Like career rates, these cards use only matches with full figures.

## Common questions
### When do career figures update?
The full figures are saved when the match ends. If the match is later corrected, the profile is updated too. See [Correct a finished match](/guides/correct-a-finished-match/) and [Fix a past ball](/guides/cricket-edit-a-past-ball/).

### Why does an older player's average look odd?
Matches scored before the detailed stats arrived only saved runs and wickets. They count in the totals, but averages and rates use only matches with full figures.
