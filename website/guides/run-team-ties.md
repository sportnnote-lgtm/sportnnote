In a team event, one fixture is several individual matches called rubbers: five singles in table tennis, three singles and two doubles in the Thomas Cup, four boards in chess. SportnNote treats it as one tie. Each rubber is a normal match you score on its own, the tie score updates as rubbers finish, and the table counts the tie once.

## Before you start
- You need to be a host of the tournament (or the tie) to create ties and enter lineups.
- Add each team's players first; the lineup picker lists them.
- Team ties work for table tennis, badminton, tennis, squash, chess, carrom, padel and pickleball.

## Step by step
1. Open the tournament, go to **Manage** and tap **New series / tie**. For a whole group or league, use **Auto-generate fixtures** instead (see below).
2. Pick the sport, **Team A (named first)** and **Team B**. **👥 Team tie (rubbers)** is already selected for these sports.
3. Under **How the tie is played**, tap a format, or **✏️ Custom rubbers** to build your own list of singles, doubles and mixed rubbers in the order they are played.
4. Under **Once the tie is decided**, choose **⏹ Stop — rest not played** or **▶️ Play the dead rubbers**.
5. For an ABC v XYZ format, choose **Who plays ABC?** or tap **🪙 Draw it**.
6. Tap **Create tie**. The tie page opens.
7. Enter each team's lineup: tap **Enter …'s lineup**, pick a player for every position (A, B, C or No.1, No.2, or each board) and the players for each doubles rubber, then tap **Save lineup**.
8. Tap a rubber to score it like any match.

> **Tip:** Each rubber gets its players once both lineups are in. Until then, the tie page shows the names only to hosts, so the referee can collect both lineups before either captain sees the other's order.

## The formats
- **Table tennis:** World Team Champs (five singles, ABC v XYZ), Corbillon (four singles and a doubles), Olympic (a doubles, then four singles).
- **Badminton:** Thomas / Uber Cup (three singles, two doubles) and Sudirman Cup (five events, mixed doubles first).
- **Tennis:** a Davis Cup tie with reverse singles, or the Davis Cup / BJK Cup Finals (two singles and a doubles).
- **Squash:** three rubbers by order of merit. **Chess:** four or six boards at once, half a point for a draw, the first-named team White on boards 1 and 3.
- **Carrom, padel, pickleball:** common club formats. Check your event's rules and use custom rubbers if they differ.

## ABC v XYZ
In table tennis, a draw before the match decides which team takes A, B, C and which takes X, Y, Z. You can change **Who plays ABC?** on the tie page until the first lineup is saved.

## When the tie is decided
A team needs more than half the rubbers: 3 of 5, 2 of 3, or 2½ of 4 chess boards.
- With **Stop**, the rubbers still to play are set aside and show **Not played**. They don't count anywhere.
- With **Play the dead rubbers**, they are played and counted, so a 3–0 can end 4–1. Group stages often do this because tie-breaks count rubbers.
- If a correction undoes the deciding win, set-aside rubbers come back.

## Every fixture as a tie
In **Auto-generate fixtures**, tap **👥 Each fixture is a team tie** and pick the format. Every pairing becomes a tie, and later knockout rounds created from the bracket are ties too.

## How the table counts ties
Each tie counts once in the table, and the **R** column shows rubbers won and lost. When you first turn on **👥 Each fixture is a team tie**, the sport's points system switches to the federation's team ranking:
- **Table tennis (ITTF):** 2 for a tie won, 1 for a tie lost; teams level go to results among themselves, then rubbers, games and points ratios.
- **Badminton (BWF), squash, carrom, pickleball:** ties won. Two teams level: head-to-head. Three or more: rubbers, then games, then points difference.
- **Tennis (Davis Cup / BJK Cup):** ties won. Two level: head-to-head. Three or more: % of rubbers, sets, games won.
- **Chess:** match points (2 a win, 1 a draw), then game points, then the direct encounter.

You can change it in the sport's points settings.

> **Note:** In a knockout, a chess match drawn 2–2 goes to the board count (board 1 counts most). If still level, use your event's rule, such as a play-off.

## Alerts and careers
- Team and tournament followers get one alert when the tie starts and one with the result (after the scorer's 60-second check), not one per rubber. A player's followers still hear about that player's rubber.
- Every rubber counts on its players' profiles like any singles or doubles match. The team's page counts the tie once.

## Common questions
### Can I change a lineup after the tie starts?
No. Once a rubber has started, the saved lineups lock.

### Can one player play two singles?
In nominated formats you'll see a warning, because most team events allow one singles each, but you can still save.

### Where do I see the tie?
In the tournament's **Matches** tab the tie is one card, **👥 Team tie · 5 rubbers**. Tap it for the tie page.
