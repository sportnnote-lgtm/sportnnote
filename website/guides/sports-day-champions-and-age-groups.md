At most school meets, each age group and gender has its own winners. U14 Boys gets a champion house and an individual champion, and so does U17 Girls. This guide shows where to find them, how ages are worked out for each age group, and how to put one teacher in charge of a single event.

## Champions by category
Once the first final in a category is locked, the sport's page in the tournament (for example **Athletics**) shows **🏆 Champions by category**, just below the house table.
1. Tap a category chip, such as **U14 Boys** or **U17 Girls**.
2. **Champion house** is the house with the most position points from that category's finals. Relays count.
3. **Individual champion** is the athlete with the most position points from individual finals in that category. Relays count for the house, not for a runner.
4. Below that you'll see the category's house table and its five best athletes, with their medal counts.
5. Tap **↗ Share all champions** to send every category's champions as one message, ready for the assembly or the notice board.

The points are the meet's own position points (8-7-6-5-4-3-2-1 unless you changed them), and tied places share them, just like the main house table.

## When two athletes have the same points
By default the athlete with more golds wins, then more silvers, then more bronzes. If they're still level, both are named joint champions, and the card says how it was decided. If your meet prefers joint champions as soon as the points are level, go to **⚙ Points & timing** (athletics) or **⚙ Pool, points & timing** (swimming), find **Champion tie-break** and pick **Joint champions**.

## Age groups: which day counts
An athlete belongs in U14 if they're 13 or younger on the cut-off day. By default SportnNote uses **31 December of the meet year**, the World Athletics and World Aquatics rule. Meets hosted by a UK or British-curriculum school start on **31 August at the start of the school year** instead, so a June 2027 meet counts ages on 31 August 2026. US meets start on 1 September. Your school association may publish its own date.
1. When adding an event, look under the athletes list for "Age groups use each athlete's age on 31 Dec 2026".
2. Tap **Change**.
3. Pick a chip. Each one shows the actual date for your meet: 31 December (marked **World Athletics**), the school-year start where your school system uses one (marked **school year start**), 1 January, **Meet day** (the meet's first day), or **Another date**. For another date, type it like `2026-10-01`. It must be the 1st or last day of a month.
4. Tap **Save cut-off**. This applies to every event of that sport in the meet.

> **Note:** This filter only decides who is offered for a category. Tap the category chip to show everyone if you need to enter someone by hand. Athletes without a date of birth are never hidden.

## Age groups beyond U14
Besides U10 to U20 and Open, a meet hosted by a school outside India also offers its own system's groups:
- **12 & Under**: 12 or younger on the cut-off day (Australia).
- **10–12 years**: at least 10 and at most 12, so younger children aren't offered either.
- **A, B and C Division** (Singapore): 18, 16 and 14 and under on 31 December.
- **Junior, Inter and Senior** (UK schools): ages 12–13, 14–15 and 16–18 on 31 August.
- **Grades 9–12** (US): uses each student's grade on the meet day from the school's class timeline, not their age. A student with no class recorded is always offered.

The line under the category chips explains the group you picked. Divisions you add when creating a tournament offer the same groups. The school system comes from the host school. See [Set up a school, its classes and graduation](/guides/set-up-a-school-and-graduation/).

## Event officials: hand one event to a teacher
Say the PE teacher at the long-jump pit should enter that event's results without being able to change the rest of the meet.
1. When adding the event, find **Event officials (optional)** just above the create button.
2. One tap adds a scorer or referee already assigned to the tournament. For anyone else, tap **＋ Someone else (mobile or name)**.
3. Create the event as usual.

To change officials later, open the event and tap **👥 Event officials** near the bottom. Edit the list, then tap **Save officials**. Only organisers can do this.

An event official can enter results, close rounds, lock the final and save any record it sets, all for their own event only. Reopening a locked round or final is still the organiser's job.

## Common questions
### Our association uses age on 1 October. Can I set that?
Yes. Pick **Another date** and type `2026-10-01`, using your meet's year.

### Why is someone missing from U14 even though they're 13 today?
They turn 14 before the cut-off day. Change the cut-off if your rules use a different date, or tap the category chip to show everyone.

### Does the champion include weightlifting bodyweight classes or both pool lengths?
Yes. A category is the age group plus gender, so all of a U17 Girls lifter's bodyweight sessions, or all 25 m and 50 m races, count towards the same championship.
