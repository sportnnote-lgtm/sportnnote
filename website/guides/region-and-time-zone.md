Kickoff times only help if they're on your clock. SportnNote starts in your phone's own time zone and works out your country from it. That decides whether a typed date like 05/03 is 5 March or May 3, whether times read 16:30 or 4:30 pm, and which day your calendar week starts on. You can change any of these in one place.

## Before you start
- Your time zone is saved to your account, so reminder emails use it too. The other choices are kept on this phone.

## Step by step
1. Open **Settings** and tap **Region & time zone**.
2. Under **Country**, tap your country. This sets the defaults for everything below.
3. Under **Time zone**, tap **Change** and pick your zone. Countries with more than one zone, like Australia and the US, list each one.
4. In another country? Tap **Other countries and zones…** to see the other countries, or search any zone by city (for example "Riyadh").
5. Under **Dates and times**, check **Typed dates**, **Clock** and **Week starts on**. A "changed" tag shows anything you changed from your country's default.
6. Under **School year and units**, check when your school year starts and whether you want metric or US units.

> **Tip:** The line under the time zone shows the time now, in that zone. Use it to check you picked the right one.

## What each setting changes
- **Time zone**: every match time, the calendar's days and "Today", and the times in reminder emails.
- **Typed dates**: how dates you type or paste are read, for example in **Import schedule** and in bulk athlete lists with dates of birth. Day first (05/03/2026 = 5 March) everywhere except the US, where month first (05/03/2026 = May 3) is the default.
- **Clock**: 24-hour (16:30) or 12-hour (4:30 pm) times on cards, Live and fixture lists.
- **Week starts on**: the first column of the calendar and the date picker.
- **School year starts**: the first academic year a school or college community starts with. A school year inside one calendar year (January start) is labelled "2026", not "2026–27".
- **Sport names and a few words**: in the US, football shows as Soccer, hockey as Field hockey, and "fixtures" as "schedule". Scores and stats are the same sport everywhere.
- **Units**: for display only. Results are always stored in metres and kilograms, so records stay comparable.

## Tournament time zones
A tournament can have its own time zone: the zone of its venue. Organisers set it in **Create tournament** or **Edit tournament** under **Time zone**. A new tournament starts in the organiser's zone.

When a tournament has a time zone:
- Kickoff times are typed in it when you schedule, edit or generate fixtures. The picker says which zone, for example "Times in UAE (GST)".
- Shared and printed fixture lists use it, and say so.
- The tournament's status (Upcoming, Live now, Completed) changes on the venue's day.
- On the tournament page, match times show in the venue's zone with its name, like "Sat, 4:00 pm GST", whenever that isn't your own zone.

> **Note:** An older tournament with no time zone shows times in each viewer's own zone, as before. Pick one in **Edit tournament** to fix it to the venue.

## Schools have their own school system
Your region also picks the **School system** a new school starts with: class names, the graduating class, age groups and the age cut-off. It is saved on the school, so everyone sees the same, and you can change it under **✎ Edit**. See [Set up a school, its classes and graduation](/guides/set-up-a-school-and-graduation/).

## Common questions
### I'm in India. Will anything change for me?
No. India keeps day-first dates, 24-hour times on cards, Sunday-first calendars and the April school year.

### My dates were read the wrong way round in an import. What do I do?
If every date in the file could be read either way, **Import schedule** asks you. Tap **Day first (DD/MM)** or **Month first (MM/DD)**. If a date like 25/10 proves the order, the app uses that order and tells you.

### I'm travelling for a tournament. Should I change my zone?
Yes, if you want times on the local clock. The tournament's own times stay in its venue zone either way.

### Why does a time show a zone name like "GST" or "AEDT"?
It means the time is in the event's zone, not yours. Times in your own zone don't show a zone name on cards.
