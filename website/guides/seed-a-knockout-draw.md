A fair knockout keeps the strongest teams apart until the end. Group winners should meet another group's runner-up, two teams from the same group shouldn't meet again straight away, and seeds 1 and 2 should only be able to meet in the final. SportnNote draws every new knockout this way. You can still keep the old pairing order if you need it.

## Before you start
- You must be an organiser (host) of the tournament.
- For a knockout after groups, finish the group matches first so the tables are final.
- Knockouts you already created stay exactly as they are. The new seeding applies to rounds you create from now on.

## Step by step
1. Open the tournament and tap **Auto-generate fixtures**.
2. For a knockout straight away, tap **🏆 Knockout (round 1)**. After a group stage, tap **🏅 Advance groups**, then **🏆 To knockout**.
3. Under **Seeding**, pick how the draw is made (see below).
4. For a straight knockout with **🥇 Seeded (list order)**, check the numbered list. The order you picked teams in is the seed order. Tap **▲ Up** to move a team up one place.
5. Tap **⚡ Generate preview**, check the matches, then tap **✅ Create … matches**.

## How teams from groups are placed
With **🥇 Standard seeding**, the app picks the right draw for your groups.
- **Top two from 2, 4, 8 or 16 groups:** a fixed cross-over. With two groups it's A1 v B2 and B1 v A2. With four it's A1 v B2 and C1 v D2 in the top half, and B1 v A2 and D1 v C2 in the bottom half. Eight groups follow the FIFA World Cup Round of 16. A group's winner and runner-up start in opposite halves, so they can only meet again in the final.
- **Any other shape:** group winners are seeded first (best record first), then runners-up, then best-placed teams. Seed 1 plays the lowest seed and seed 2 starts at the bottom of the draw. If two teams from one group would meet in round 1, the app swaps a lower seed with another match where it can.
- **Draw order (old):** the order used before. Use it only if you need to match a draw made earlier.

## Seeding a straight knockout
- **🥇 Seeded (list order):** standard seed positions. With eight teams that's 1 v 8, 4 v 5, 2 v 7 and 3 v 6. Seeds 1 and 2 can only meet in the final, and the top four can only meet from the semi-finals.
- **🎲 Random draw:** every place is drawn at random.
- **Draw order (1v2, 3v4):** teams play in the order picked, with no seeding.

## Byes when the numbers don't fit
A full draw needs 4, 8, 16 or 32 teams. With any other number, the top seeds get a bye into the next round. Everyone else plays round 1, seed against seed from the other end of the list. With six teams, seeds 1 and 2 have byes and 3 v 6 and 4 v 5 play. Seed 1 then meets the winner of 4 v 5. The preview tells you how many byes there are and which round they go into.

> **Note:** Each knockout match now remembers its place in the draw. If you move a match to another day, the winners still meet the right opponents in the next round.

## Best-placed teams when groups are different sizes
Say 22 teams are split into groups of 6, 6, 5 and 5. A team in a group of 6 plays one more match, so comparing total points isn't fair. When the groups differ in size, the app compares best-placed teams per game played. It looks at points per game first, then goal (or score) difference per game, then goals (or points) scored per game. A note under the qualifiers says so. When the groups are the same size, nothing changes: totals are used.

> **Tip:** If your competition's rules use a different method, such as ignoring results against the bottom team, tap **✏️ Pick manually** and choose the qualifiers yourself.

## Common questions
### Can I set the seeding before the groups finish?
Yes. Open the sport's settings and under **Seeding** choose **Standard** or **Draw order**. The fixtures screen starts from that choice next time.

### Our seed 1 and seed 2 met in the semi-final. Why?
That knockout was created before this change, or with **Draw order**. Earlier rounds keep their pairings. Rounds you create from now on use the seeding.

### Does seeding work with team ties?
Yes. Each tie takes one place in the draw, just like a single match.
