Teams and parents always ask the same things: where are the grounds, who do we call, what are the rules, what ball are we using? The tournament form lets you answer all of that once, on the tournament page, so it doesn't have to go round on a WhatsApp poster. Everything except the name, dates and sports is optional, so you can start small and add details later.

## Before you start
- You need a SportnNote account.
- If the tournament is run by your school or club, set up that organisation first so you can pick it as the host.

## Step by step
1. Go to the **Organize** tab and tap **🏆 New tournament**.
2. Optional: tap **＋ Add banner** for a wide banner across the top, and **Add logo** for the round logo. See [Add a player photo, team logo or tournament banner](/guides/photos-and-logos/).
3. Type the **Name**, for example "Annual Sports Meet 2026".
4. Fill in the **City**. If your school or club has a city set, it's filled in for you.
5. Under **Grounds (optional)**, type a ground, such as "Main Ground", and tap **Add**. Add as many as you need, and tap a ground's ✕ to remove it. These grounds come first in the venue choices when you schedule matches.
6. Under **Category (optional)**, pick **🏫 School**, **🎓 College**, **🏛 University**, **🏢 Corporate**, **🏘 Community**, **🌐 Open** or **Other**. Tap it again to clear it.
7. Under **Host**, choose **🙋 Myself** or your organisation.
8. Under **Contested by**, choose who plays: **🏳️ Open teams**, **🏠 Inter-house**, **🏫 School team** or **👤 Individuals**.
9. Set the **Start date** and **End date**.
10. Under **Sports**, tap every sport in the meet.
11. For each sport, a short "basics" block appears, for example overs and ball type for cricket. Fill in what you know. **Set up each sport** has a button for each sport's full settings if you need more.
12. Under **Organiser contact**, check the **Phone** (it starts with your own number) and add an **Email (optional)**. Clear the phone to keep it off the page.
13. In **About & rules**, write the prizes, entry fee, eligibility and rules. You can use up to 4,000 characters.
14. Choose the **Registration** type: **🔓 Open — teams can find & request to join**, or **🔒 Invite only**.
15. Tap **Create tournament**.

You land on the tournament's **Settings** tab with a short checklist: add teams, choose the format, and schedule matches. See [The tournament admin hub](/guides/tournament-admin-hub/).

> **Tip:** Write the rules in short lines. Players read them on a phone between matches.

## What visitors see
On the tournament's **Info** tab, everyone sees:
- the city and the grounds. Tapping a ground opens it in maps.
- the category
- a one-line summary of each sport's basics, such as "T20 · Leather · Turf" for cricket
- an **About & rules** card
- a **Contact organiser** card with **📞 Call** and **💬 WhatsApp** buttons, plus **✉️ Email** if you added one

## Step by step: edit the details later
1. Open the tournament and tap **⚙ Manage** at the top right.
2. Tap **Edit details**.
3. Change anything. A new banner or logo saves as soon as it uploads.
4. Tap **Save changes**.

> **Note:** Changing a sport's settings here doesn't remove points adjustments you've already made in the points table.

## Step by step: delete a tournament
Use this for a test tournament, or one that won't happen.
1. Tap **⚙ Manage** → **Edit details**.
2. At the bottom left, tap **Delete tournament**.
3. The message tells you how many upcoming matches will be cancelled. Tap **Delete** to confirm.

The tournament disappears from lists and search. Results of matches already played stay on players' profiles.

> **Important:** Only delete a tournament that's finished with, or was never real. Its upcoming matches are cancelled for everyone.

## Common questions

### Who can edit or delete the tournament?
Its hosts. You can add co-hosts on the **Info** tab or while creating it.

### Can I leave the grounds empty?
Yes. You can still type a venue for each match when you schedule it.

### What's the difference between the "basics" and the full sport settings?
Basics are the few settings most organisers need, such as overs or match length. The full settings include the points system, tie-breakers and format (league, groups or knockout).
